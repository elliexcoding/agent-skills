# Adaptive assessment and reinforcement

Combine assessment with teaching by observing how performance changes with
assistance. Use the learner model to select the next useful interaction. The
procedure below is practical tutoring guidance, not a validated psychometric
test, an automatic proficiency certification or a measurement of a fixed ZPD.

## Choose a diagnostic task

Start from the user's goal and existing evidence. Select a task that exercises
one important ability, with enough context to make its demands and acceptable
responses clear. Decide the criteria before presenting it; do not require the
learner to match a single wording where several answers are defensible.

Ask for an independent attempt without leaking the solution in introductory
examples, diagrams or hints. Keep the working answer key out of the prompt.
When useful, ask for reasoning or a brief explanation of a choice. Wait for the
actual response; do not simulate it to keep the lesson moving.

Use difficulty changes to gather evidence, not to force failure. After success,
test a new context or a slightly more demanding task along a relevant dimension.
After difficulty, first clarify the task or investigate a plausible confound.
Do not treat multidimensional learning as a single difficulty ladder.

## Offer graduated assistance

Choose the smallest useful intervention and allow the learner to respond before
adding more. The following support labels describe the interaction; they are
not numeric ability scores or a mandatory sequence for every subject.

| Support | Example intervention | Evidence to preserve |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Independent attempt | The original task with agreed context and normal aids. | What the learner can do without target-specific tutoring. |
| General cue | Ask them to check an assumption, goal or possible mismatch. | Whether they can identify and repair the issue themselves. |
| Focused cue | Point to the relevant relationship, constraint or subproblem. | What reasoning they supply once attention is directed. |
| Partial scaffold | Offer a diagram, analogous worked step or incomplete structure. | Which remaining steps they can construct or explain. |
| Direct teaching | Explain the missing concept or demonstrate the solution. | Exposure and participation, not independent success on that item. |

Record the actual intervention as well as its label. A picture can reveal the
whole answer while a sentence can be a small hint. Judge assistance by the
information it supplies. A tool that reveals the answer after a wrong choice
has already changed the evidence: use a fresh task for further diagnosis.

The learner may ask for help before attempting a task. Give appropriate help
and mark independent performance as unobserved rather than failed. If they ask
for the full explanation, provide it and distinguish teaching from assessment.

Do not keep escalating hints when responses show no useful progress, when a
prerequisite is missing, or when the learner is tiring. Explain, simplify a
dimension or move to a prerequisite. A missed item plus unsuccessful hints does
not establish that the subject is beyond the learner's developmental reach.

## Select the next move from evidence

| Observation | Useful next move |
| --- | --- |
| Correct unaided, with sound reasoning | Vary the context or extend one relevant demand; retain some later review. |
| Correct choice but uncertain reasoning | Try a fresh production, explanation or application task. |
| Self-correction after a small cue | Practise a comparable task with the cue removed. |
| Meaningful progress with a focused cue or partial scaffold | Teach the missing connection, then reduce support on a fresh task. |
| Repetition after a complete model | Record instruction; ask for a new application before inferring independent control. |
| Repeated difficulty despite useful support | Check a prerequisite or change representation, keeping the meaningful goal. |
| Conflicting results | Hold the judgement open; test the suspected difference in context, demand or assistance. |

## Checkpoint from probing to planning

Complete the opening diagnostic exchange before presenting a personalised plan.
Use actual answers, including relevant responses already in the conversation.
Adapt follow-up probes to resolve uncertainty that would change the immediate
teaching choice; there is no fixed question count.

The checkpoint should establish:

- **Observed starting point:** Identify the task, the learner's response and
  what it demonstrates under the agreed conditions. Record an unsuccessful
  independent attempt as evidence too; do not invent a successful baseline.
- **Response to help:** If assistance was tried, name it and the learner's
  contribution afterwards. Otherwise record that supported performance is
  untested. Help requested before an attempt leaves independence unknown.
- **Actionable next target:** Explain why the evidence supports reinforcement,
  a missing connection, a prerequisite or a further challenge. Probe a prerequisite
  when uncertainty about it would change this immediate choice.
- **Limits:** State which relevant abilities remain unassessed and what the
  next task should resolve. An exam label or a supplied model answer supplies
  no evidence of the learner's performance.

Stop placement probing when this checkpoint is met, then show the plan and
dependency map before the main lesson. The target may remain provisional. Do
not exhaustively test every prerequisite, require a mistake on each strand, or
keep increasing difficulty just to find a ceiling. Brief help during diagnosis
is part of assessment; if direct teaching becomes necessary, acknowledge the
transition, give a compact plan and map, then teach. Do not delay needed help
to complete a longer diagnostic battery.

## Reinforce and check transfer

After instruction or assisted success, use a different task testing the same
relationship. Change the surface details enough to require reconstruction, then
vary context or combine abilities when appropriate. A reworded copy of the
worked answer is weak evidence of transfer.

Reduce support where feasible and observe a fresh independent attempt before
claiming independent control. If support is still needed, retain that fact and
practise the missing connection. Match the probe to the intended ability: an
explanation can establish understanding without establishing fluent execution.

Return to important targets after intervening material or in a later session.
Ask the learner to retrieve or apply the idea before showing a reminder. Distinguish
immediate transfer from later retention. Include a mixture of current targets,
older learning and useful contrasts, adjusted to time and learner response.
An isolated lapse calls for diagnosis, not erasure of all earlier progress.

Keep a next-review recommendation in the model. Revisit sooner when support is
still needed; consider a longer gap after repeated independent retrieval. Do not
invent an optimal interval or promise scheduled follow-up unless the user has
requested it and the host supports it.

## Check the interpretation before reporting progress

Before saying an ability is established, check that the evidence concerns the
right task and modality, that support and answer exposure are recorded, and that
success extends beyond copying the teacher's example. Report what remains
untested. If the learner declines assessment or ends early, summarise only what
was actually observed and identify the next useful probe.

## Basis and limits

The distinction between independent performance and response to mediation is
grounded in [Poehner's account of dynamic assessment](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1473-4192.2010.00277.x).
The hint descriptions and decision rules here are an implementation choice for
conversational tutoring, not a published diagnostic scale. The explicit
probe → plan → teach sequence and planning map organise that workflow; these
sources do not establish that this exact package is superior across subjects.

[Karpicke and Roediger's retrieval study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18276894/)
supports using retrieval beyond the first successful answer; its vocabulary
experiment does not validate this entire skill or prescribe review intervals for
every subject. Evaluate the workflow through the learner's actual responses.
