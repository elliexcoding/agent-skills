# Worked assessment examples

These examples show how to apply the same learner model and assessment loop
across subjects. They are fictional teaching scenarios, not observations about
the current learner. Use only the example that helps with the present task.

## Choosing a topic for exam preparation

Suppose the learner says, "I want to prepare for an advanced exam; pick a point
to practise." The request gives a goal and authorises topic selection; it does
not demonstrate ability. Choose a provisional focus relevant to that goal,
checking uncertain syllabus claims, and ask a contextual diagnostic question.
End the opening interaction there and wait for the answer.

Adapt the next probe to what the learner actually does. Once the evidence
supports a teaching choice, explain that choice and draw the prerequisite →
target → application map, with the observed and untested areas distinguished.
Then teach and check a fresh application. The map might connect understanding
the context and a relevant contrast to choosing a form, then applying it in a
new passage; the concrete nodes depend on the topic and responses.

If asked to save a note before the learner answers, label it as a worksheet with
assessment pending and one next action. Do not fill in a learner profile or
present the provisional topic as a diagnosed need. If the learner requests only
a worksheet, provide that instead of requiring an interactive session.

## Advanced language use

Suppose a learner wants more nuanced participation in professional discussion
in a language they already use well. Treat "advanced" as reported background,
not proof of equal performance in every ability. Select a meaningful task at
that starting level, rather than defaulting to beginner vocabulary.

Separate relevant targets such as interpreting implied stance, expressing a
qualified disagreement, connecting an argument, or adjusting register to a
specified audience. Distinguish comprehension, production and the modality
actually observed. Choose only the strands needed for the user's purpose.

**Initial task:** Give a short, contextualised passage in the target language
and ask the learner to explain the author's reservation and write a response to
a specified colleague. Do not supply a translation or model response first.
Assess fidelity to meaning, the response's reasoning and its suitability to the
stated relationship. Use current sources to check uncertain language judgements;
accept alternatives justified by context or variety.

**Illustrative observation:** The learner interprets the reservation accurately
but their reply unintentionally sounds categorical. Their reading evidence does
not cancel the production difficulty or establish a global language level.

**General cue:** Ask whether the reply conveys the degree of certainty they
intend. If this helps, ask for a revision and record what changed.

**Focused cue if needed:** Direct attention to the distinction between
acknowledging the colleague's concern and qualifying the learner's own claim.
Do not immediately supply the finished wording.

**Partial scaffold if needed:** Offer an outline of the communicative moves or
contrast two unrelated examples. Record what the learner then contributes.
If direct modelling is needed, teach explicitly and mark that answer as exposed.

**Fresh probe:** Use a different topic with comparable demands. Remove the cue
and ask for another response. Later, change the audience or communication mode
to investigate transfer, keeping the changed conditions explicit. Revisit the
target after other practice before claiming retention.

An illustrative model update could be:

| Target | Evidence | Interpretation | Next action |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Interpret a reservation in this written genre | Accurate explanation before help on one passage. | Independent evidence, limited sample. | Try a contrasting passage later. |
| Express a qualified disagreement in writing | Revised appropriately after a focused cue. | Supported evidence in this context. | Fresh response without the cue. |
| Spontaneous spoken disagreement | No audio interaction. | Untested. | Assess through audio only if relevant and available. |

Keep grammatical correctness, pragmatic suitability and stylistic preference
distinct. Do not equate success on a closed-choice grammar question with
spontaneous production, or infer pronunciation from typed language. In a text-only
host, continue useful written practice and leave audio-dependent targets open.
Do not assign a formal proficiency level from these informal exercises.

## Mathematical reasoning

Suppose the goal is understanding why one operation preserves an equation's
solutions and another can introduce invalid candidates. Begin with an unaided
comparison of two transformations and ask the learner to justify their choices.

If a learner can calculate but cannot explain equivalence, keep those as separate
targets. A general cue might ask whether the step is reversible. A focused cue
can direct attention to finding two different inputs with the same output. A
partial scaffold can provide one case and ask for a counterexample. After
teaching, use a different equation and ask the learner to check candidate
solutions independently. Do not count copying the earlier calculation as proof.

A diagram may help explain a many-to-one relationship, but record it as support
if it supplies the key insight during assessment. Later success without that
support is additional evidence; success with a normal reference sheet may still
be independent under the agreed conditions.

## Technical reasoning and practical work

Suppose the learner can describe an indexing technique but wants to choose it
appropriately for a real workload. Ask for a prediction and justification using
a small concrete workload, with the relevant data and constraints supplied.

Correct terminology alone establishes little about choosing or verifying an
implementation. If the learner overlooks a workload constraint, direct attention
to it before supplying the design. After guided analysis, vary one important
constraint and request a fresh choice. If practical execution matters, separately
observe an implementation or an interpretation of actual results when the host
supports it. A correct written prediction does not demonstrate measured system
performance or successful implementation.

## Cases that should change the workflow

- **The user asks for an explanation without quizzes:** Teach from the stated
  background, offer examples, and leave independent performance unassessed.
- **A quiz tool immediately reveals the answer:** Record answer exposure; use
  conversation or a fresh item to investigate the effect of graduated help.
- **The learner succeeds on one item and struggles on another:** Compare the
  demands and context before revising the interpretation of the whole ability.
- **The learner asks to continue in another harness:** Provide or save the
  evidence snapshot as requested. The next session should recheck a relevant
  target, not treat the snapshot as permanent mastery.
